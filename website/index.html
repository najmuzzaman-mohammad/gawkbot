Never leave your agents hanging.

gawkbot finds the coding agents installed on your Mac, turns them into a team, and gives them, and the ones you run on a cloud computer, one place to ask you things. When one needs you, it comes out of the notch. Answer with a key, or just say it.

Inbox

One inbox for every agent's questions.

Agents stop and ask, and the question sits in a tab you closed an hour ago. gawkbot runs your agents as a team and collects every question they ask in one list, so nothing sits there waiting while you look somewhere else.

  • Tagged at a glance. Each question shows which agent asked, the tool that runs it, and whether it runs on this Mac or on a cloud computer.
  • Answer in place. Approve, reject, pick an option or type a reply, right there in the panel.
  • Nothing through your accounts without you. Emails, posts and every other action through an account you connected wait for your yes, one at a time. Reads don't have to.

Voice

Talk back with your voice.

Some answers are quicker said than typed. Hold V, say it, let go. gawkbot shows you what it heard, and nothing goes to the agent until you press ↵.

  • On-device on the Mac. The Mac app uses Apple's speech recognition, on-device when your Mac supports it.
  • A draft, not a send. Fix a word before it goes, or press Esc and it never happened.

iPhone

Reply from your iPhone, even to agents running in the cloud.

Walk away from the desk and the questions come with you. Pair your phone once by scanning the code in Access & Health, with the phone on a network that can reach your Mac (your Wi-Fi or Tailscale), then answer with a tap, a few words or the mic.

  • Every agent in one list. The ones on your Mac and the ones in the cloud, with the same faces as on the desktop.
  • Same one-tap answers. Approvals and choices arrive as cards with buttons.
  • Hold to talk. The big button at the bottom does what you would expect.

The iPhone app is new and builds from source with Xcode, from apps/ios in the repo. It is not on the App Store, and push notifications are not in yet.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is gawkbot free?
Yes. There is no subscription, no seat count, no trial and no usage fee. You bring the agents and API keys you already use. The source is public under the Sustainable Use License: free to self-host for your own internal business purposes and for non-commercial use.
Which agents does it work with?
Claude Code, Codex, Opencode, Gemini CLI, Cursor CLI, Aider, Goose, Amp, Qwen Code, Copilot CLI, Crush, Factory Droid, Ollama, OpenClaw and Hermes. Each one is tagged with the tool that started it and whether it runs on your Mac or in the cloud.
Do I need a MacBook with a notch?
No. On a Mac without a notch the same strip shows as a pill at the top centre of the screen, with the same panel, shortcuts and sounds.
Is there an iPhone app?
Yes, and it is new. It lives in the repo under apps/ios and you build it with Xcode; it is not on the App Store. Pair it once by scanning the code in Access & Health, with the phone on a network that can reach your Mac (your Wi-Fi or Tailscale). Push notifications are not in yet.
Where does my data go?
gawkbot runs on your machine and your workspace is local files you can read. Release builds include anonymous product analytics (counts and shapes, never your content) and, unless you turn it off, session replay that masks everything you type but can show what is on screen. Switch either off in onboarding or Settings. A build from source has no analytics key and sends no analytics; the only call home is the update check.
Can an agent do something external without asking me?
Not through your connected accounts. Reads are free, but an email, a post or any other action through an account you connected waits in the notch until you approve it, one action at a time, unless you choose always-allow for that one action. An agent working in a repo on your machine still uses the tool's own permissions.
How does voice work?
Hold V and talk. In the Mac app speech recognition uses Apple's speech framework, on-device when your Mac supports it; on the iPhone it is the same. You see the transcript first, and nothing is sent until you press ↵.
Does it work on Windows or Linux?
The web app does: run npx gawkbot on macOS, Windows or Linux and it opens in your browser, and there is a desktop app for Windows. The notch itself is Mac only.
