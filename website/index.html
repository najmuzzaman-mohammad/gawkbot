THE BOTS

They do the boring part.

You find out afterwards, on a dashboard.

Every bot runs on your machine as its own worker, with a shell, a browser, and a working directory of its own. It uses the tools you allow it, nothing more, and then it does the thing you have been putting off since March.

  • filer Filed forty-one expense reports overnight. Left you a chart to look at.
  • reconciler Matched the invoices against the bank. Two did not agree. Both now do.
  • triager Triaged your inbox before you woke up. Ninety-three down to four.
  • scheduler Booked the six meetings you kept not booking. Invitations are out.
  • chaser Chased everyone who owed you a reply. Got four. Still working on Dave.
  • tidier Renamed nine hundred files to the convention you described once, badly.

PERMISSIONS

Trust your bots with less over time.

This is the part that is not a joke. A bot with your credentials and a browser is a real risk, and the honest posture is to grant it as little as possible for as long as possible. The defaults below are the actual defaults.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY DOES
  • Runs on your machine, with your keys. There is no gawkbot cloud in the path, and no account to create. Point it at a local model and nothing leaves at all.
  • Every send, commit, purchase, and delete is gated behind an approval you have to click. Approval is per action, not a blanket grant, and it does not persist.
  • Each bot works in its own directory with its own tool allowlist, so what a bot can touch is per bot rather than per workspace. Your credentials never leave your machine.
  • Every tool call is written to a local log you can read. If a bot did something, there is a line for it.
  • Permissions shrink by default. A grant you stop using expires rather than accumulating quietly.
OPEN SOURCE GROK BOT

The open source Grok Bot.

gawkbot is an open source Grok Bot: a team of always-on AI bots that runs on your own machine instead of a vendor's cloud. It is free, it uses the coding bot you already pay for (Claude Code, Codex, or a local model), every external action waits for your approval, and it builds you a microapp so you have something to look at.
